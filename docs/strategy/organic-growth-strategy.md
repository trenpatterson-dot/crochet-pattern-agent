# Organic Growth Strategy

## Positioning

Crochet Pattern Agent is for beginners who are tired of "easy" crochet patterns that are not actually easy.

The product should compete by reducing beginner friction, not by claiming to have the most patterns or by making crochet sound effortless.

## Audience

- New crocheters who get stuck on basic pattern steps.
- Beginners who have tried a few tutorials and still feel confused.
- People helping a beginner learn, including friends, partners, parents, or relatives.
- Left-handed beginners who struggle with right-handed tutorials.
- Beginners who need realistic first projects, plain-language material guidance, and clear finishing help.

## Content Pillars

- Pain point posts: honest questions about magic rings, losing stitches, hand pain, yarn splitting, confusing abbreviations, left-handed gaps, finishing, and project overwhelm.
- Beginner education: simple explanations of yarn weight, hook size, stitch counts, turning chains, abbreviations, and finishing steps.
- Tool demos: show how Crochet Pattern Agent can warn about friction, explain materials, surface beginner-friendly tutorials, or suggest smaller first projects.
- Community feedback: ask what actually makes a pattern beginner-friendly and turn replies into product notes.

## Feedback Loop

1. Ask one specific beginner-friction question.
2. Track replies in `content/community-engagement/reply-insights.md`.
3. Group repeated themes by pain point.
4. Convert repeated confusion into content ideas, feature labels, filters, or prompt updates.
5. Keep the wording honest: "people said this was confusing" is better than pretending the tool already solves everything.

## How Community Replies Become Product Ideas

Community replies should become product ideas when multiple people describe the same friction in practical terms.

Examples:

- If people say magic ring tutorials move too fast, add a "No Magic Ring" filter or warning label.
- If people say their rows keep shrinking, add stitch-count checkpoints and stitch marker reminders.
- If people say the yarn looked pretty but was hard to use, add yarn-splitting warnings and beginner yarn notes.
- If left-handed beginners say they cannot follow right-handed videos, prioritize left-handed tutorial labels.
- If people say they finished the stitches but did not know how to finish the item, add finishing checklists.

## Competitor Intelligence

Competitor intelligence should use the beginner pain-point rubric from `docs/strategy/beginner-pain-points.md`.

Evaluate crochet websites, blogs, YouTube tutorials, Pinterest-style discovery, pattern platforms, beginner crochet tools, Etsy, and Ravelry by how well they help with:

- Splitting Yarn
- The Magic Ring
- Losing Stitches
- Tension Issues
- Pattern Language
- Hand Pain
- Yarn Selection Confusion
- Left-Handed Frustration
- The Finishing Gap
- Project Overwhelm

The output should show what competitors do well, which pain points they address, which pain points they miss, where the beginner experience is confusing, and what Crochet Pattern Agent can improve.

## What Not To Do

- Do not fake crochet expertise.
- Do not pretend the builder is the crocheter.
- Do not hype the tool as a finished solution for every beginner problem.
- Do not pretend crochet is easy for everyone.
- Do not use buy-now language.
- Do not make unsupported claims about popularity, pricing, conversion, or results.
- Do not shame beginners for getting stuck.
